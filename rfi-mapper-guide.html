RFI Mapper Tester Guide

A browser-based version of the wide-area power-line noise mapper described in QST, December 2026. Drive your streets with a radio and a GPS and get a color-coded noise map.

Open RFI Mapper
Looking for testers. The radio and GPS links have only been checked against simulated data and reply parsers so far, not against real hardware in a car. Tell me what works and what doesn't (last section).

What you need

  • Laptop with Chrome or Edge (Windows, Mac or Linux) and two free USB ports. Web Serial is required, so Firefox, Safari and phone browsers can't talk to the radio.
  • HF radio with CAT/CI-V control and a data cable to the laptop. Supported: Icom IC-706MkIIG (CI-V address 58), other Icom CI-V radios, Yaesu FT-991A class radios, Kenwood TS-590 / TS-480 class radios. The IC-706MkIIG is the one tested by the author.
  • Mobile HF antenna on the vehicle for a high band such as 10 m.
  • USB GPS receiver that outputs NMEA, for example a GlobalSat BU-353S4 (4800 baud). A USB extension cable lets you put it on the roof. Use a quality shielded USB cable, since unshielded ones generate their own RFI.
  • A quiet vehicle. Check your own vehicle noise first. S5 or lower with the engine running is good. Ferrites on the USB cables near the laptop help.
  • Optional: a Google account for Google My Maps, and a passenger to run the laptop.

Radio setup

  1. Set the radio to an unused frequency on a high band, around 28.650 MHz.
  2. Mode AM, AGC fast, noise blanker off.
  3. Set the radio's CI-V or CAT baud rate to match the app's Baud dropdown. IC-706MkIIG default in the app is 19200; set it on the radio under SET → CI-V Baud.
  4. Close any other program using the radio's serial port (logger, WSJT-X, etc.). Only one program can hold it.

Running a survey

  1. Open the page, choose your radio, check Baud and CI-V address, click Connect radio, and pick the radio's serial port. A live S-meter value should appear. If it says "No reply", the baud rate or address is wrong.
  2. Under GPS choose USB GPS (NMEA), set 4800 baud, click Connect GPS, and pick the GPS port. A first fix can take up to a minute. A laptop's built-in browser location is not accurate enough and shouldn't be used for real runs.
  3. Plan a route that covers every public road within the area you care about: about half a mile for wire antennas, up to a mile for beams. Pick a quiet time such as a weekend morning, and avoid thunderstorms because lightning gives false readings.
  4. Raise the speaker volume, click Start logging, and drive 20 to 30 mph. A sample is saved each second. The voice announces the level whenever it is above S9 and every 10 seconds as a heartbeat. You should hear "S 0" or similar when it starts.
  5. When finished click Stop logging, then Save CSV.

Safety. Don't look at the screen while driving. Put the laptop and radio where you aren't tempted, or have a passenger run it.

Reading the map

Each dot is one sample, colored by the strongest S-meter reading since the previous GPS fix. Click a dot for details. Click a legend row to highlight only that level. Above S9 each S unit is 6 dB, so S11 is 12 dB over S9.

Green areas rule out noise, so you only need to investigate the hot spots. Anything above about S10 close to your own house deserves a closer look.

After the run

  • Share it: import the CSV into Google My Maps (Import, then pick Latitude and Longitude). The columns are Sample, Time, Latitude, Longitude, Elevation, Sats, S-Meter, Speed_mph.
  • Confirm the pole: go to the hot spot with a directional VHF Yagi, an attenuator and a handheld, and compare what you hear with your home station.
  • Talk to the power company with screenshots of an overview map and a close-up of the suspect pole. Never touch power-pole hardware.
  • Re-drive the route after repairs to confirm the noise is gone.

Try it without hardware

Choose Simulator for both the radio and the GPS, click both Connect buttons, then Start logging. A simulated car circles a block with one noisy pole. You can also use Open CSV… to view any file with latitude, longitude and S-meter columns.

Known limits

ItemStatus
IC-706MkIIG, other Icom, Yaesu, Kenwood S-meter readParsers checked against sample replies only. Real-radio reports needed.
S-meter calibrationS9 is assumed at raw value 120 (Icom, Yaesu) or 15 (Kenwood). Adjust the "Raw at S9" box if your meter disagrees with a known signal.
GPSNMEA over USB serial at 4800 to 115200 baud. The app doesn't change the GPS baud rate itself.
Phones and tabletsMap and CSV viewing work. USB radio and GPS access do not (no Web Serial).
Fixed-station long recordingNot built yet.

What to report back

  • Radio model, cable or interface, baud rate, and whether the S-meter value appeared and tracked a signal.
  • GPS model, baud, and how long to first fix.
  • Anything that errored, plus a sample of your CSV if the map looked wrong.
  • Whether the colors lined up with noise you could hear.
